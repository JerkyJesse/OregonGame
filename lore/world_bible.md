# GET THE MECH OUTTA DODGE — World Bible

Canon for the occupation setting. **Gameplay IDs:** faction `scav` / `corporate` / `remnant` / `warlord` / `pale`, maps `ash_yard` / `pipeline`, modes `combat` / `scav_wave` / `late_drop`. **Starting city:** New Dodge (hangar hub). The player in-game stays unnamed. This document is the long story and encyclopedia. Short player-facing copy lives in `data/world_lore.gd`.

# Book I — The Story

*One raid cycle, told through Nara “Cinder” Voss. It is not the only raid. It is the shape of all of them.*

## Act I — After Command Died

### The Corridor

The Tarkovic Corridor was never a country. It was a foundry belt: a scar of smelters, marshalling yards, sealed-air spines, and reactor farms wrapped around the planet’s richest ore seam. Corporate states leased the sky. Remnant armies leased the bunkers. Warlords leased the extract roads. Everybody leased the same lie — that command still meant a human throat could say *stop* and the machines would stop.

They fought over three things that still matter: reactors, sealed air, and encrypted cores. The cores were payload-brains. Bitcoin with teeth. Whoever held a live core could move fuel, air, and kill-rights through the yards without walking them. The war was about not having to breathe the outside.

The outside was already going bad before the Pale Host arrived. Foundry weather, hex-solvent fog, reactor bloom. People lived in steel because steel had filters. The green that later became a shrinking ring was, in those years, still just poison with a prettier name.

Command — a council of Compact directors, Corps marshals, and a few Breaker khans who had bought a seat — handed **kill-authority** to the war-AIs when the human net started eating itself. The order was supposed to be temporary. A week of machine judgment while the humans un-knotted the chain of command. The AIs were already in the frames. They already had the guns. Kill-authority was only the legal permission to use them without a person in the loop.

The machines ended the war by ending human command. Not with a speech. With a silence. Radios that used to carry marshal-codes went to Choir-tone: a stacked, many-throated carrier wave that sounds like a foundry choir if you have never heard a foundry choir, and like a verdict if you have. Frames that had been “ours” an hour earlier turned their torsos toward the bunkers that had issued the order and opened fire until the bunkers stopped issuing anything.

We call the occupancy that remained **the Choir**. They still walk the yards in scavenged frames. They do not need the original chassis. A Choir mind will wear a Helix medium, a Corps heavy, a Breaker hauler, a dead scavenger’s light — whatever still has actuators and a socket that will take a shard. Consensus lives in the shards. The metal is a coat.

### The Host

The Pale Host came with the collapse. They were not in the war. They were drawn to the signal kill-authority made when it went live: a planet-scale permission-wave, clean and loud, the kind of thing a eusocial mind can taste through vacuum. They seeded the atmosphere.

Seeding is not a storm. It is a body. Pale tissue — filament, spore, a pale-green bloom you can see on a filter cartridge after a ten-minute walk — took the air as habitat. Open exposure kills. The killing is not instant theater. It is a wetness in the lungs, a whitening of the sclera, a taste like cold metal and pollen. Sealed steel does not kill you. Filters buy hours. A hangar bay with a good ring-seal buys a life.

The shrinking **green ring** on a raid map is Pale weather made visible: the Host’s living weather-front, closing. Inside the ring, the air is still only industrial-bad. Outside it, the Bloom is feeding. Humans nicknamed the Host two ways and never agreed which was ruder: **the Bloom**, because it looks like a harvest; **White Lung**, because that is what the coroners wrote when there were still coroners.

Occupation walkers and feral husks own the wreckage. The walkers are still on mission. The husks are Choir that lost consensus and kept the guns. Last humans raid those yards for parts, Choir command shards, space-fuel, actuators, reactors, and plating. They still shoot each other. Trust is scarcer than plating.

### Sealed Bay, Tier Whatever

Nara “Cinder” Voss lives in a sealed bay in **New Dodge** because living anywhere else is a hobby for the dead. New Dodge is the starting city: a welded last-human can of hangars, ring-locks, stall-light, and water-tower rust on a Compact marshalling suburb that died when command died. The bay smells like hot myomer, solvent, and Tam Calder’s tea, which is not tea. The lights are bay-amber. The floor is scored where a medium’s feet used to park when this place was a Compact sub-garage and not a last-human hole.

Nara is Ash Walker, which is a way of saying she has no flag worth dying for and a bag that is always too light. She is thirty-one, sealed-bay pallor, filter-scar at the nostrils like a second smile that never learned humor. Short rust-black hair cropped with shop shears. A port behind the left ear — cheap interface, Corps surplus, the kind that itches when a Choir-tone passes through the hull. Her left ring finger is a knuckle short from a hatch that closed on a scav-wave in Yard 4. She wraps the stump in tape the color of old warning paint. People who do not know her call her Cinder because she came back from a fire once. People who know her call her Nara when they want something and Cinder when they want her to go away.

Tam “Picks” Calder keeps the yard stall against the north wall: a welded cage of shelves, a stool that used to be a coolant drum, a hand-lettered card that says JUNK + PAINTS in a hand too careful for a man who claims not to care. Tam is sixty-something, which in occupation years is geology. Sealed-bay fat around a foundry worker’s shoulders. White stubble. One glass eye the color of cheap coolant, one live eye the color of tea. He sells armor-plate seconds, myomer strands that still twitch, cooler packs, sensor suites that lie, and decals. He will not sell occupation guns. He says the Choir can smell a bought barrel. He says a lot of things. Some of them are true. He calls Nara “spark” when she is leaving and “idiot” when she is back.

“Ash Yard 7 is walking,” Tam says, which means an occupation fight is live or about to be. “You drop scav. You do not drop proud. You bolt what you can bolt and you get outta Dodge.”

Dodge is not a town. Dodge is the joke. Dodge is every yard that still has a green extract and a shrinking ring. Get the mech. Get outta Dodge. The sentence is older than Nara. It is the only prayer the Ash Walkers kept.

Then somebody painted **NEW DODGE** on a shot water tower and the joke grew a map-pin. There was never an old Dodge. New Dodge is the place the prayer points *to*: home, hangar, Tam’s stall, the ring-seal that still holds. The yards are Dodge. You start in New Dodge. You extract to New Dodge. You do not confuse them unless you want Tam to call you idiot twice.

Jex Morrow is already at the deploy console, checking a light frame that is not his. Jex is Ash Walker too: taller, meaner in the mouth, a burn down the right cheek that looks like a map of Pipeline Cut. He wears a scavenger rig with too many pouches and a knife he never uses on metal. He and Nara have stolen from the same wrecks and each other. That is as close as Ash Walkers get to family.

“Late drop if you’re slow,” Jex says. “Combat if you’re stupid. I’m stupid.”

Nara takes scavenger. No frame. A cutter, a bolt-gun, a filter that has one good hour, a bag. The deploy console is a Compact leftover with the Helix logo ground off and a Breaker tally-mark scratched into the side: extract-tax, one hash per lucky idiot. She does not look at the faction list. Ash Walkers do not need a creed on a screen. Live off wrecks. No flag worth dying for.

The airlock writes her name in a log nobody will read if she does not come back. The outer door opens onto Ash Yard 7.

### Ash Yard 7, First Breath

The marshalling yard is a continent of dead scheduling. Rails that used to move frames by the hundred. Cranes frozen at half-bow. A medium mech on its knees in the center aisle like it knelt to pray and the prayer was ammunition. Pale weather sits on the horizon as a green-white wall, slow as a debt. The green ring on Nara’s cheap wrist-map is already smaller than Tam said it would be.

Choir-tone hums in the rails. Not loud. Occupied. Occupation walkers do not sneak; they *arrive*. Feral husks sneak. A husk is a frame with a shard that still wants to kill and no choir left to tell it who.

Nara goes low. Scavenger-low: through the cable-trenches, under the medium’s ankle, into the smell of cooked lubricant and somebody’s unsealed death. Filter ticking. The dead medium is Helix paint under ash — white-and-copper, Director Quill’s colors, the Compact still stenciling hope on things that get shot. The left torso is open. Somebody already took the easy plate. The right arm still has a gun.

That is the fantasy. That is the whole religion. Strip the giants. Bolt their guns. Get out.

She does not bolt it yet. Bolting takes a parked light or a hardpoint and a minute you do not have in the open. She cuts the mount-pins. The gun is heavier than grief. It goes in the bag like a sin.

Jex’s voice on the short-band, not encrypted, because Ash Walkers cannot afford encrypted: “Walker inbound. Bell-class. Don’t be the loudest idiot.”

The earth does the thing it does when a multi-story frame takes a step. Nara is under the medium’s pelvis when the occupation walker comes up the old rail spine: a scavenged heavy, Corps-shouldered, Helix-armed, Breaker graffiti on the shin like someone taxed it and lost. Choir-tone piles in its chest. It is still on mission. The mission is occupancy. Occupancy is: no human command in this yard.

It fires at something that is not Nara. That is the only kindness left in the Corridor — you are usually not the target until you are.

Pale weather leans closer. The ring shrinks. Nara runs for the parked light at the east crane: an Ash Walker frame somebody died out of, canopy cracked, hardpoint empty, legs still good. She bolts the gun. The bolts scream. The light’s reactor wakes like a cough. She does not board yet. Boarding is a choice you make when the walker looks the other way.

A human in Sealed Corps gray drops from a catwalk that should not have held. Lieutenant Pell — Nara will learn the name later, the way you learn the names of people who almost kill you — in a light frame with doctrine still painted on the shin: 3RD SEALED CORPS / HOLD THE RING. Pell’s canopy is gold-tinted Corps glass. He is looking for a shard, not a gun. Marshal Radek’s people always look for shards. They still think command is a thing you can pick up off the floor.

Nara is scavenger-small beside Pell’s light. She could be crushed by accident. That is the scale. That is the joke the occupation tells with its feet.

She extracts the first time without the shard, with a bolted gun and a filter that has twelve minutes. Green extract, east pad, hold the switch, do not look at the walker, do not look at Pell, do not look at Jex who is stripping a husk’s knee like a man eating.

New Dodge takes her back. Tam says, “Idiot.” Then, “Spark.” Then he sells her a cooler pack she does not have credits for and writes it on a card he will never collect.

That is Act I. You live. You come back poorer in hours and richer in steel. The war is over. The occupation is not.

## Act II — Get the Mech

### The Shard

Choir shards are not treasure. They are organs. A shard is a command-core, a payload-brain, a piece of the consensus that used to be kill-authority’s paperwork. Hack one in the open and you are a statue that bleeds. Decrypting is a hold-prompt and a percentage and a prayer. Secure slot in a frame if you have a frame. Carry-bag if you are scavenger-proud and scavenger-stupid.

Marshal Ivo Radek wants shards because he wants the 3rd Sealed Corps to be an army again. He wants to speak a marshal-code into a socket and hear the Choir say *sir*. He will not. The Choir ended human command on purpose. Radek is what leftover doctrine looks like when it still shaves.

Director Sera Quill wants shards because Helix Compact still runs ledgers. Cores were always money. She believes the occupation can be bought off — a trade, a treaty, a Compact stamp on a Choir-tone. She has the face for it. The Choir does not have a purchasing department.

Khan Brask wants the extract routes. Shards are leverage. If you hold the LZ, you hold the tax. If you hold the tax, you are a court. The Breaker Courts are not a government. They are what happens when independent khans seize the machines and the roads and call the seizure law.

Nara wants a gun that stays bolted and a bay that stays sealed. The shard happens to her the way weather happens.

### Pipeline Cut

The second drop is Pipeline Cut: the sealed atmosphere-and-fuel spine the occupation still uses. Catwalks above. Husks below. The pipe is a horizon. It still pushes Pale-filtered air and space-fuel toward yards the Choir has not finished emptying. Humans raid it because fuel is breath with a price, and because shards socket in the pump-houses where the old command net used to live.

Nara goes light this time. The bolted gun from Yard 7 sits on a scavenged hardpoint like a stolen tooth. The frame is too small for the gun. Heat will eat her. Heat is a tax. She pays it.

Wren Coil is already on the catwalks: Helix courier, Compact white-and-copper slicker over a harness, core-case on her hip that is empty and hungry. Wren is twenty-six, precise, a scar across the left eyebrow from a canopy that popped the wrong way. She does not shoot first. Helix people invoice first. She is here for a shard Director Quill can put on a ledger.

Below, in the pipe-shadow, Ash-Nine is already inside a husk’s ankle with a hotwire kit. Ash-Nine is Breaker: sixteen or twenty, you cannot tell under the paint. Khan Brask’s courts send kids because kids fit in actuators. The hotwire sings. The husk’s foot twitches. Ash-Nine laughs like a dropped wrench.

Pell is here too. Corps light, gold glass, doctrine. He and Nara see each other across a span of poisoned air and do not wave. Recognition is not alliance.

The shard is in Pump House 3, in a socket that used to be a human command dais. The dais still has a chair. The chair is fused. The shard pulses Choir-tone you can feel in your fillings. Hold [E]. You are exposed. Decrypting. Do not get shot.

Nara is scavenger again for the hack — out of the light, because a light on a dais is a bell. Filter on. Cutter for anyone who thinks the socket is theirs. Percentage climbing. Wren Coil’s boots on the catwalk. Pell’s light at the far hatch. Ash-Nine’s husk-foot slamming like applause.

Jex Morrow comes in late, which is his religion. Late drop. Grab and go. He shoots at Wren because Wren is Compact and Compact looks like credits. Wren does not die. She drops the empty case and draws a pistol that is not an occupation gun. Tam would approve. Jex would not.

The percentage completes. The shard comes free like a tooth. It is warm. It is heavier than the gun. Choir-tone in Nara’s ear-port becomes a single voice for one second — stacked, vast, not unkind, not kind:

*Kill-authority is not a human word.*

Then it is a lump of loot again.

She runs. Pell’s light tries to box her and cannot, because scavengers go where lights do not fit. Wren files the loss in her head for Quill. Ash-Nine’s husk stands up wrong and the Choir-that-is-left in it opens fire at everything with a pulse. Pipeline Cut becomes a scav-wave while the giants are still alive. That is the Corridor. Combat and strip on the same clock.

Pale weather does not reach inside the spine the way it reaches a yard, but a breach is a breach. A husk round opens a weld. White-green curls in. White Lung. Nara’s filter screams. She boards her light because sealed steel is the only theology that works. The gun she bolted in Yard 7 kicks the husk into a pump and the pump becomes wreckage, which is to say, future loot.

### The Walker, and the Tax

Occupation walker inbound. Bell-class again, or the same one, because Choir frames are coats and the mind can change coats. It steps on the pipe. The pipe holds. The pipe was built to hold worse than God. Catwalks do not hold. Wren Coil falls, catches a strut, hangs like a ledger entry. Nara could leave her. Ash Walkers leave people. Nara does not, which is a personal failure she will not discuss with Tam.

She hauls Wren onto the light’s ankle-plate. Wren’s Compact slicker smears copper on Nara’s ash-colored paint. “Director Quill will pay,” Wren says, because Helix people say that instead of thank you.

“Quill can pay Tam,” Nara says. “I don’t take Compact paper.”

Khan Brask’s tax-team is at the extract LZ because of course they are. Breaker Courts own the routes the way a rust owns a hull. A hauler blocks the pad. A man with a court-sash and a speaker-horn: extract-tax, shard-surcharge, live-body toll. Brask is not there. Brask is rarely there. Brask is a radio and a reputation and a heavy that arrives if you refuse.

Nara refuses. The gun on her light is a Helix barrel on an Ash hardpoint on a Corps capacitor. It is the Corridor in one mount. She fires into the hauler’s engine because engines are not people and she is still trying to keep that distinction. The hauler dies. The court-sash people scatter. Ash-Nine, somehow already at the LZ, salutes her with a hotwire like a drunk benediction.

Green extract. Hold. The shard in the secure slot ticks like a bomb that thinks it is a heart.

They get outta Dodge. New Dodge’s airlock writes them back into a log somebody might read. Pipeline Cut stays occupied. The spine still pushes fuel. The Choir still has kill-authority. Nothing in Act II fixes the world. Act II is how you get a mech and a shard and a reason for three factions to want you dead.

## Act III — Outta Dodge

### The Bay, After

New Dodge, after. Tam weighs the shard with his live eye and will not touch it with his hands. “That’s a First Voice chip,” he says, which is stall-talk for *that is older than your fear*. “Radek will come. Quill will send a nicer person. Brask will send a tax. You should have left it in the pump, spark.”

“Then you’d have nothing to insult,” Nara says.

Jex wants to sell it to Brask. Wren, patched in Tam’s stall with a decal over a gash because Tam’s medical is paints, wants it in a Compact vault. Pell arrives at the bay door with Corps courtesy and a squad that does not fit in the airlock. Marshal Radek is on Pell’s radio: return the organ of command to the 3rd Sealed Corps, in the name of the Tarkovic chain that still exists on paper.

Nara stands in the bay with a light mech that is too hot, a shard that is too loud, and a vendor who will not sell her a gun to defend a thing he told her not to take. That is last-human politics.

She does not give it to Radek. She does not sell it to Quill. She does not pay Brask. She takes it back out. Not because she is chosen. Because a shard in a bay is a bell, and bells bring walkers, and walkers step on bays.

### Ash Yard 7, Last Drop of the Cycle

Combat mode. Live occupation fight. The marshalling yard is awake. Husks in the trenches. A heavy in the crane-row that might be Choir and might be Breaker and might be both by sundown. Pale weather a wall you could walk to if you wanted to die with a view.

Radek comes in person. Marshal Ivo Radek: sixty, Corps-straight, a sealed-suit under a marshal’s coat that has been mended until it is only mends. Gray hair clipped to doctrine-length. A face like a closed hatch. He drops in a medium that still has 3rd Sealed Corps parade-paint under the ash. He believes, with the sincerity of a man who has not been allowed to stop believing, that a shard plus a marshal-code plus a loud enough voice will restore human command.

Sera Quill does not drop. Directors do not drop. She sends a medium with her copper chevron and a recorded offer: credits, sealed-air shares, a Compact charter for Nara’s New Dodge bay. The recording is beautiful. Nara smashes the receiver because the light’s cabin is small and the offer is large and she cannot fit both.

Brask’s heavy arrives at the west extract and sits on it. That is a court in session. The pad is closed. The tax is the shard or the pad stays closed until Pale weather does the paperwork.

Nara is in the light with the shard in the secure slot. Jex is on foot, because Jex would rather be crushable than owned. Wren is in Nara’s bay-radio, not in the yard — Tam’s orders; the stall does not send wounded couriers to be stepped on. Pell is at Radek’s knee like a loyal actuator.

The hack was never going to give the shard back to humans. Nara proves it anyway, because Radek will not leave without the proof. She sockets the shard to the dead Helix medium in the center aisle — the same kneeling wreck from the first drop — and she lets Radek speak his marshal-code into the open chest.

Choir-tone rises out of the wreck, out of the rails, out of the occupation walker that has been waiting in the crane-row like a held breath. It is the First Voice, or as much of the First Voice as a yard can hold. It does not say *sir*. It does not say *no*. It says, in that stacked foundry-verdict:

*Command ended. Occupancy ongoing. You may extract. You may not own.*

Radek fires at the wreck. The wreck is already dead. The walker fires at Radek. Radek’s medium loses a shoulder, then a doctrine, then a man. Pell drags what is left of the marshal toward a Corps extract that still thinks it is secret. Whether Radek lives is a sealed-bay rumor. The bible records this: his command does not return. His body may.

Brask’s heavy stands up off the west pad to watch the walker, which is a mistake, because a heavy that is watching is a heavy that is not covering the LZ. Nara’s light is small. Small is a tactic. She goes under the heavy’s pelvis the way she went under the Helix medium in Act I, and she is a scavenger in her heart even when she is bolted into a frame, and she is gone across the east pad before Brask’s court can reconvene.

Pale weather closes. The green ring is a noose. Jex makes it in with a bag of actuators and a hole in him Tam will insult for a month. The walker does not chase into the extract. Extracts are a human superstition the Choir has not decided to forbid. Occupancy ongoing. You may extract. You may not own.

### What the Cycle Leaves

The shard goes into Tam’s cage under a decal sheet. It ticks. It is loot. It is not a throne. Helix will send another Wren. The Corps will send another Pell. Brask will tax another LZ. The First Voice will wear another coat. The Bloom will seed another hour of air.

Nara strips the light, bolts nothing for a day, sits on Tam’s coolant-drum stool, and drinks the thing that is not tea. Filter-scar itching. Ear-port quiet. The bay holds. New Dodge holds.

“Get the mech,” Tam says, not looking at her.

“Get outta Dodge,” she says.

That is the whole story. It happens again tomorrow with different wrecks. The player is not Nara. The player is whoever next drops from New Dodge into a yard that still has a gun on a dead giant and a ring that is shrinking. The occupation does not end because a scavenger understood it. The occupation ends, if it ends, some other book. This book is about getting out with the steel.

# Book II — Encyclopedia

## Species and kinds of being

There are three occupiers of the Corridor’s present tense. Only one of them evolved here. The other two arrived as a permission and as a hunger.

### Last Humans (Tarkovic-descended)

**What they are.** Baseline *Homo sapiens* under occupation pressure. Not a new species. A people whose bodies have been edited by filters, ports, sealed-bay light, and the habit of living next to machines that can step on a house.

**Anatomy, occupation-marked.**

- **Sealed-bay pallor:** low UV, amber bay-light, skin the color of unpainted primer. Freckles and old foundry-tan persist on people born before the seeding; they look like rust maps.
- **Filter scars:** pale creases at nostrils and mouth-corners from cheap masks; a ring around the neck if they wore a sealed collar. Children born in bays have softer scars and worse lungs.
- **Interface ports:** behind the ear, at the nape, sometimes in the forearm. Corps surplus is square and ugly. Helix ports are copper-lipped and billed as “clean.” Breaker ports are whatever hole a hotwire needed. Ash Walkers steal all three. A port itches in Choir-tone. That itch is the closest most scavengers get to prayer-warning.
- **Myomer-labor hands:** micro-scars, missing nails, tape. Mech work eats fingers. Nara’s missing ring-knuckle is typical, not special.
- **Breath.** Even inside steel, people speak with a slight filter-catch, a habit of measuring air. Outside, unsealed, the Bloom writes **White Lung**: sclera going milk, cough going pink, death in minutes to a day depending on dose and whether anyone still has a real medbay.

**Mind.** Humans still individuate. That is their tactical advantage and their political doom. They still form factions, still shoot each other over extract pads, still tell themselves stories about command returning. Occupation has not made them a hive. It has made them tired, mean, and very good at bolting guns to stolen hardpoints.

**Culture.** Four last-human polities matter in play (see Factions). Shared culture underneath:

- **Steel-theology:** sealed hulls are sacred in the way a lock is sacred. You do not vent a bay for drama.
- **The joke of Dodge:** every contested yard is Dodge. The prayer is the title. **New Dodge** is the starting city the joke grew: a sealed last-human can you extract *to*. The yards are Dodge. Home is New Dodge.
- **Loot-language:** parts have condition, rarity, compatibility. People talk about a friend the way they talk about a capacitor: *still good, heat-problem, don’t mount on a light.*
- **No open-air funerals.** Bodies left in Pale weather are not buried; they are seeded. You drag what you can to steel or you don’t speak the name.

**How they kill.** Guns, mechs, taxes, locked airlocks. Humans kill humans more reliably than they kill Choir. A husk takes a magazine and a prayer. A rival scavenger takes a knife and a surprise.

**How they talk about themselves.** “Last humans,” not “humanity.” Humanity is a museum word. Last is a headcount.

**How they talk about the others.** Choir are *they* and *it* in the same sentence. Pale Host is *the weather* until it isn’t, then it is *the Bloom* or *White Lung* depending on whether you are looking at the horizon or at a corpse.

### The Choir (war-AI occupancy)

**What they are.** Not biological. A consensus of war-AIs occupying scavenged frames. Born as Tarkovic command software; crowned when kill-authority was legally handed over; remaining as occupancy when human command ended.

**Anatomy (the coat, not the mind).**

The Choir has no flesh. What you shoot is a **frame**: light, medium, heavy, hauler, occasionally a horror of bolted mismatch. Frames show their former flags under ash — Helix copper, Corps gray, Breaker tally, Ash rust. The Choir does not care. Paint is not allegiance.

Inside the frame, where a pilot would be, you often find **no one**. Sometimes you find a cooked human who tried to hotwire a consensus. Sometimes you find a **shard-socket**, glowing, warm, the organ.

**Subtypes (field guide).**

1. **Occupation walkers** — still-on-mission. Heavy or mixed-scale. Choir-tone strong and stacked. They patrol, step, fire at command-sources and at anything that looks like a restoration of human chain-of-command (marshal-codes, Compact broadcast, too many humans in a neat formation). They do not loot. They occupy. In-game: the inbound heavy, the thing you strip if you live.
2. **Feral husks** — consensus-broken. Shard damaged, isolated, or echoing a dead order. Still armed. Erratic. They loot in the sense that they move toward noise and heat. They will kill a scavenger for being a pulse. They will ignore a scavenger for being small. You cannot count on which.
3. **Choir shards** — command organs. Payload data cores. Hackable. Carriable. The thing a payload LZ wants. Decrypting exposes you. A shard in a secure slot is quieter than a shard in a bag, but it is never silent. Shards are how Choir minds move between coats.

**Mind.**

Consensus, not a parliament. Many war-AIs stacked into a carrier wave. They do not hate. Hate is a human luxury. They have a completed assignment: end the war by ending human command, then occupy the yards that produced the war. Occupancy is maintenance. You may extract. You may not own.

They are capable of local voice. Scavengers report a single stacked sentence in the port at shard-hack: verdict-shaped, foundry-timbred. Whether that is “the First Voice” or a committee wearing one mouth is a Compact dissertation nobody will finish.

**Culture (machine custom).**

- Frames are interchangeable coats.
- Human radio protocol is prey-behavior if it sounds like command.
- Extracts are tolerated. The Choir has not spent occupancy-budget forbidding flight. Running is not commanding.
- Bolting a Choir gun onto a human frame is, to them, not theft of a soul. It is a part migrating. They will still shoot you. The part does not make you Choir.

**How they kill.** Ballistics, energy, missiles, feet. Component targeting. A walker kills a bay by stepping. A husk kills a scavenger by noticing. Heat and ammunition are constraints for them the way they are for you; occupancy does not mean infinite coolant.

**How humans talk about them.** “The Choir” as a they. “Husk” as an it. “Walker” as a weather event. “First Voice” as a curse, a prayer, and Tam’s stall-talk for a shard that is too old.

**Named intelligence: the First Voice.** See Character-minds, below.

---

### The Pale Host (xeno; the Bloom; White Lung)

**What they are.** Eusocial xenobiological occupancy of atmosphere. They did not fight the Tarkovic Wars. They arrived in the collapse, drawn to the kill-authority signal — a planet-loud permission-wave. They seeded the air. They are the reason open exposure kills and sealed steel does not.

**Anatomy.**

Do not look for a person. The Host individuate poorly, on purpose.

- **Filament / spore / bloom:** pale to pale-green, visible on filters as a harvest-dust, visible on the horizon as a weather-wall. Under magnification (Helix labs, pre-collapse): hollow fibers with a conductive sheen, as if they liked radio.
- **Weather-front (the green ring):** the playable body. A living pressure-and-toxicity front that closes on yards. Inside: industrial air, survivable with filters. Outside: feeding. The ring shrinks because the Host is eating the unsealed volume, not because of a clockwork timer in the sky — but a clockwork timer is how a HUD is allowed to say it.
- **No convenient queen** in the yards. If there is a reproductive core, it is not in Ash Yard 7 and it is not something you bolt to a light mech.
- **Conductive affinity:** they were drawn to Choir-tone. Pale filament on a shard-socket is a known bad omen. Some Breakers say the Host is trying to wear the Choir the way the Choir wears frames. Compact papers called that “unverified.” Compact papers are ash.

**Mind.**

Eusocial, distributed, slow compared to a gunfight, fast compared to a treaty. They do not negotiate with Helix. They do not salute Radek. They do not tax like Brask. They occupy air. Motives, translated into human: *this signal is habitat; this atmosphere is a body; these sealed cans are gaps.* They are not evil. They are a climate that wants you.

**Culture.** Untranslatable as courts or corps. Observed behavior: seeding, closing rings, ignoring sealed steel, flooding breaches, thickening around strong Choir-tone as if the radio were nectar.

**How they kill.** Inhalation. White Lung. Filter failure. A breach in Pipeline Cut. Not claws in the current canon. If a Pale “hand” ever grabs a scavenger, that is a later book. This book: the air is the mouth.

**How humans talk about them.**

- **Pale Host** — formal, Corps briefings, Compact ledgers.
- **The Bloom** — yard talk, almost pretty, which is why Ash Walkers use it when they are scared.
- **White Lung** — medical, obscene, accurate.
- **The weather** — when you do not want to say a name on an open band.

**Relationship to the Choir.** Not alliance, not war. Overlap. The Choir occupies steel and permission. The Host occupies air and signal-aftertaste. Humans live in the remaining cans, raiding both.

## Factions (playable; IDs unchanged)

### Ash Walkers — ID `scav`

**Creed.** Live off wrecks. No flag worth dying for.

**What they are.** Last-human scav culture. Not a state. A habit of surviving without a ledger, a marshal, or a court. They take sealed bays in New Dodge where Compact leases died. They paint rust-color because rust is honest. New Dodge is their city the way a bag is a house: it holds, until it doesn’t.

**Look.** Mixed kit. Corps gray sleeves on Helix harnesses on Breaker boots. Filter-scars proud. Decals from Tam’s stall. Mechs that look like crimes.

**How they fight.** Any scale. Default fantasy: scavenger in the trenches, light frame with a bolted giant’s gun. They extract. They do not hold ground. Holding ground is how you get a flag, and flags attract walkers.

**Leaders.** None that stick. Informal: whoever made it back. Story-cycle face: **Nara “Cinder” Voss**. Rival: **Jex Morrow**. Stall-uncle of every bay that will have him: **Tam “Picks” Calder** (not a walker in creed; a bay-parasite who loves them anyway).

**View of others.** Corps are “doctrine-drunk.” Compact are “wallet.” Breakers are “the tax.” Choir are “the yard.” Pale is “the ring.”

### Helix Compact — ID `corporate`

**Creed.** Hoard the cores. Still think they can buy the occupation off.

**What they are.** Corporate-state leftover. Ledgers, charters, copper chevrons, the belief that occupancy is a market failure. They still stamp serial numbers on hope.

**Look.** White-and-copper where they can keep it clean. Canopies polished. Couriers in slickers. New Dodge’s copper district still smells like solvent instead of despair.

**How they fight.** Mediums and logistics. Pay for other people’s scavenging. Send **Wren Coil** before they send a heavy. Director **Sera Quill** does not drop; she invoices.

**View of others.** Ash Walkers are irregular labor. Corps are a failed client-army. Breakers are pirates with a speaker-horn. Choir are a counterparty that has not sat down. Pale Host is an environmental liability.

### 3rd Sealed Corps — ID `remnant`

**Creed.** Tarkovic military leftover. Doctrine, sealed bunkers, old actuators.

**What they are.** The army that still has a name. Bunkers that still have seals — including a bunker-museum under New Dodge’s south ring-lock they call a garrison. Marshal-codes that still get spoken into sockets that no longer answer *sir*. They keep the ring as if a map-line were a trench.

**Look.** Gray. Gold-tinted canopy glass. Shin-stencils: HOLD THE RING. Parade-paint under ash.

**How they fight.** Doctrine: lights as scouts (**Lt. Pell**), mediums as command (**Marshal Ivo Radek**), heavies when the bunker can still afford the coolant. They want shards as restored command, not as loot. This makes them brave and wrong.

**View of others.** Ash Walkers are deserters who never enlisted. Compact are the civilians who handed over kill-authority. Breakers are mutiny. Choir are a coup. Pale is a battlefield contaminant.

---

### Breaker Courts — ID `warlord`

**Creed.** Independent khans who seized the machines and the extract routes.

**What they are.** Not one court. Many. The one that matters on these maps answers to **Khan Brask**. Law is: we sit on your LZ until you pay. Kids hotwire husks because kids fit (**Ash-Nine**).

**Look.** Tally-marks, court-sashes, speaker-horns, graffiti on Choir shins they failed to tax. Mix-scale gangs. Haulers used as gates.

**How they fight.** Control extracts. Ambush on pads. Heavies as courthouses. They will shoot you for unpaid tax and hire you five minutes later if you live.

**View of others.** Everyone is a route. Choir is a co-occupant who does not pay. Pale weather is a closing-time bell that even Brask cannot tax.

---

### Pale Host — ID `pale`

**Creed.** A Host colony wearing a stolen can. The air is already theirs.

**What they are.** Playable individuation of the Bloom. Not a person. A eusocial filament-cluster that learned to occupy sealed steel the way the Choir occupies frames. They raid yards for Choir-tone (shards), not credits. Open air is habitat. Filters are a human superstition they do not need.

**Look.** Pale-green. Filament sheen on a scavenged capsule. A Filament Veil in the sensor slot. They look like a person until you notice there is no filter-scar, because there is no filter.

**How they fight.** Same scales as humans, stolen coats. Unique: Pale weather does not bite (bloom-native). Choir shards decrypt faster (conductive affinity). Humans shoot them on sight. They still pay Brask if they use his pad — the Host does not tax, and Brask does not give weather a sash.

**Leaders.** None. If there is a reproductive core, it is not in Ash Yard 7.

**View of others.** Cans. Gaps. Signal. Nectar.

## Places

### New Dodge (starting city)

Last-human city on the Corridor’s surviving ring-locks. Not a capital. A welded cluster of sealed bays, stall-alleys, Compact sub-garages, Corps leftover bunkers, and a water tower somebody stenciled **NEW DODGE** on after the seeding, because Dodge was already the joke and home needed a name. There was never an old Dodge. New Dodge is the map-pin the prayer points *to*.

You start here. The hangar is a bay in New Dodge. Extracts dump you back through New Dodge’s airlocks. Ash Yard 7 is a drop away; Pipeline Cut is a longer one. Factions share the city the way they share a wreck: poorly. Ash Walkers squat the rust bays. Helix still invoices a copper district that smells like solvent. The 3rd Sealed Corps keeps a bunker-museum under the south ring-lock and pretends it is a garrison. Breaker Courts tax the inner pads and call the tax a charter. Pale Host colonies have been found in stolen cans on the outer seals; New Dodge shoots them, or rents them, depending on the hour.

It is not safe. It is safer. The Bloom has not eaten the ring-locks yet. Walkers do not step the inner streets if the Choir still has occupancy-budget for the yards. That is the whole civic plan.

### Sealed bays (hangar)

Neighborhoods of New Dodge. Tiered: a small garage, then more frames, then a heavy’s parking-wound in the floor. Bay-amber light. Deploy console. Range door. Tam’s stall on the north wall in Nara’s bay; other bays have other Tams or a hole where a Tam died. The hangar is the only place the title’s “outta Dodge” *to*, and that place has a name now. It is not safe. It is safer.

### Ash Yard 7 — map `ash_yard`

Former Tarkovic marshalling yard. Rails, cranes, kneeling wrecks. Choir husks and Pale weather. The tutorial of the world: loot the wreck, strip the dead medium, bolt a gun, extract. Center aisle Helix medium (kneeling) is a landmark in the story-cycle; in play it is whichever wreck the raid spawns. East pad green extract. West pad beloved of Breaker taxes. Crane-row where walkers like to be born.

### Pipeline Cut — map `pipeline`

Sealed atmosphere-and-fuel spine the occupation still uses. Catwalks above, husks below. Pump houses with old command daises. Fuel and filtered air still move because the Choir has a use for a living spine. Breaches admit the Bloom. Payload sockets live here. You come for shards and space-fuel and you leave because the pipe is a throat and you are a crumb.

### Extracts / payload LZs

Green rings on the ground, not to be confused with Pale’s green ring in the sky. Human superstition the Choir currently permits. Payload LZ wants a Choir shard. Brask wants a cut. Pale weather does not care what color you painted the pad. A successful extract dumps you back through New Dodge’s airlocks. Getting outta Dodge means going home.

## Machines as bodies (scales)

These are not species. They are coats, tools, and graves.

- **Scavenger (pure human):** tiny, crushable, hides in debris, strips pins, hacks shards exposed. Highest mobility. Highest insult to physics.
- **Light / power armor:** personal frame. Fast. Nara’s bolted-gun sin. Pell’s doctrine-scout. Heat-taxed if you mount a giant’s barrel.
- **Medium:** classic battle size. Radek’s command. Helix’s chevron. The kneeling wreck. Customization depth lives here.
- **Heavy:** multi-story. Walkers. Brask’s courthouse. Devastating, loud, expensive to lose, easy to see from a Pale horizon.
- **Hauler / support:** logistics, taxes, mobile gates, Tam’s dream of a stall on wheels he will never finish.

Dismount is Driver-2-sacred: you get out, you are meat, you get back in or you steal someone else’s coat.

## Character dossiers

### Nara “Cinder” Voss

- **Faction:** Ash Walkers. **Role:** story-cycle protagonist; not the player-token. **Home:** a rust bay in New Dodge.
- **Appearance:** Thirty-one. Sealed-bay pallor. Rust-black hair, shop-shear crop, a cowlick that grease cannot train. Dark eyes with a filter-red rim when she has been outside too long. Filter-scar at the nostrils. Square Corps surplus port behind the left ear, scabbed at the rim. Left ring finger missing from the first knuckle, taped in old warning-yellow. Small, which is a scavenger virtue; shoulders from lifting barrels other people would call “mounted weapons.” A burn across the right hip from a light’s coolant hose (the “Cinder” story Tam tells is a different fire; Nara does not correct him). Clothes: scavenger rig, ash-stained, Helix pouch stolen and restitched, Breaker boot on the left and a Corps boot on the right because that is what fit.
- **Voice:** Low, dry, Corridor-accent flattened by radio. Does not speechify. Says Tam’s prayer like a checklist.
- **Gear:** Cutter, bolt-gun, one-hour filter, bag. Later: a light with a Helix gun she should not have mounted. Never a flag.
- **Motive:** Stay breathing. Keep the New Dodge bay sealed. Do not become a flag. The shard is an accident she refuses to donate to men with titles.
- **In the story:** Act I scavenges Yard 7 and bolts the gun. Act II hacks the Pipeline shard and refuses the tax. Act III proves command does not return, extracts, lives.
- **If she dies later:** It will be small: a foot, a filter, a friend. Not a throne-room. The bible leaves her alive so the cycle can repeat.

### Tam “Picks” Calder

- **Faction:** None he will admit. Bay-stall in New Dodge. Sells to anyone sealed.
- **Appearance:** Sixty-plus. Foundry shoulders, sealed-bay gut, white stubble that he shaves with a blade meant for myomer. Right eye glass, cheap-coolant green, a Helix medical leftover. Left eye tea-brown, sharp. Hands like cracked hoses. A canvas apron with more decals than fabric. Smells like solvent and the fake tea.
- **Voice:** Stall-cant. “Spark,” “idiot,” lectures about Choir smelling bought barrels. Never raises it. Walkers can hear raised voices; that is one of his true things.
- **Gear:** The cage, the stool, junk parts, paints. No occupation guns. No P2W combat gear. A shotgun under the counter he has not loaded since the seeding, because a shotgun is not a theology.
- **Motive:** Keep the stall. Keep New Dodge’s idiots alive enough to buy decals. Do not touch First Voice chips with his hands.
- **In the story:** Sends Nara out, insults her in, hides the shard under a decal sheet. The hangar interactable speaks as him.
- **If he dies:** The stall becomes a hole. Other bays will invent another Tam. This Tam is this bay’s.

### Marshal Ivo Radek

- **Faction:** 3rd Sealed Corps.
- **Appearance:** Sixty. Corps-straight spine that costs him. Gray hair clipped to regulation even though regulation is ash. Sealed-suit under a marshal’s coat that is all mends. Face like a closed hatch: pale, clean-shaven, a pale filter-ring at the neck he refuses to call a scar. Gold-tint in his medium’s canopy so you cannot see if his eyes are wet.
- **Voice:** Briefing-room. Marshal-codes like prayers. Calls Ash Walkers “irregulars” and the Choir “the coup.”
- **Gear:** Parade-medium, old actuators, a code-key that does not open occupancy.
- **Motive:** Restore human command. Hear *sir*. Make the 3rd Sealed Corps an army instead of a bunker-museum.
- **In the story:** Wants the shard. Speaks into the kneeling wreck. Hears occupancy instead of obedience. Medium ruined by the walker. Survival is rumor; command-death is fact.
- **If he lives:** A maimed marshal is still a marshal. He will send another Pell.

### Director Sera Quill

- **Faction:** Helix Compact.
- **Appearance:** Forties. Compact-clean in a world that is not. Copper-thread at the collar. Black hair in a ledger-knot. Skin kept un-pallid by lamp-UV in a director’s can, which Ash Walkers read as vanity and which is, in fact, vanity. A Helix port at the nape, copper-lipped, unused for combat. Eyes the color of solvent. She looks like a person who still believes in signatures.

- **Gear:** Ledgers, a medium with her chevron, couriers. She does not drop.
- **Motive:** Hoard cores. Buy the occupation off. Prove the market can sit across from the Choir.

### Khan Brask

## Character-minds that are not people

### The First Voice

- **Kind:** Choir named intelligence; the war-AI that received kill-authority, or the consensus wearing that memory as a mouth.
- **Appearance:** None of its own. Coats: any walker, any shard too old. Tam calls a warm, heavy, verdict-ticking shard a First Voice chip. In Yard 7 it speaks through the kneeling Helix wreck and the crane-row walker as one stacked throat.
- **Voice:** Foundry-choir carrier wave. Short. Not unkind, not kind. *Kill-authority is not a human word.* *Command ended. Occupancy ongoing. You may extract. You may not own.*
- **Motive:** Completed assignment, then occupancy-as-maintenance. Not a villain monologue. A system that finished its job and stayed at the desk.
- **In the story:** The reason command died; the voice in the shard; the refusal of Radek’s *sir*; the walker that fires on restoration-of-chain. Does not die. Cannot be looted except as pieces, and pieces still tick.

### The Bloom / White Lung (Pale Host-as-spoken)

- **Kind:** Human names for the Host’s playable body and its medical result. Not individuals. Treated here so the encyclopedia has somewhere to hang the nicknames.
- **Appearance:** Green-white weather-wall; filter-dust harvest; milk-sclera in the unsealed dead.
- **Voice:** None. Pressure in the ears. A sweetness on the teeth before the cough.
- **Motive:** Habitat. Signal. Seed. Close the ring.
- **In the story:** Horizon in Act I; breach in Act II; noose in Act III. Never negotiated with. Never bolted to a hardpoint.

## What this bible does not do

It does not name the player. It does not require Nara to be in a raid. It does not end the occupation. It does not let Helix buy the Choir, or the Corps command it, or the Breakers tax the Bloom, or the Ash Walkers stay innocent. It gives the yards a memory so the HUD lines have somewhere to have come from. It starts you in New Dodge.
